# Rent arrears and possession: the position for secure tenancies

For a secure tenancy, the council may recover possession for rent arrears only under
Ground 1 of Schedule 2 to the Housing Act 1985, and only where the court is satisfied
that it is reasonable to make the order. Arrears alone do not found a claim: Ground 1 is
discretionary, so the debt establishes the ground but the court retains a full discretion
on reasonableness and on whether to postpone or suspend. This briefing states the law as
at 1 July 2026.

## Statutory basis

Ground 1 is made out where rent lawfully due from the tenant has not been paid, or an
obligation of the tenancy has been broken. It is one of the grounds in Part 1 of Schedule
2, which the court may rely on under section 84 only if it also considers it reasonable to
grant possession. The reasonableness requirement is the operative constraint, not the
arithmetic of the account.

## The section 83 notice

Proceedings must be preceded by a notice of seeking possession under section 83, in the
prescribed form, specifying Ground 1 and the particulars relied on. The notice is valid
for 12 months from the earliest date on which proceedings may begin. A defective or
expired notice cannot be cured at trial; the court may dispense with notice under section
83(1)(b) only where it is just and equitable to do so.

## Reasonableness and the terms of the order

Because Ground 1 is discretionary, the court weighs the conduct of both parties, the
cause of the arrears, the tenant's payment record and any live claim for Universal Credit
or Housing Benefit. Where it makes an order, the court will ordinarily suspend it on terms
under section 85, typically current rent plus a sum toward the arrears. A suspended order
does not end the tenancy; breach of its terms does, subject to the tenant's right to apply
for further postponement while the order remains suspended.

## Pre-action steps

The Pre-Action Protocol for Possession Claims by Social Landlords applies. The council
must have contacted the tenant, provided the arrears figure — for example £1,240 as at 1
July 2026 — considered the tenant's circumstances, and made reasonable attempts to agree
an affordable arrangement before issuing. The court will expect the protocol to have been
followed and may adjourn, or make an adverse costs order, where it has not.

## What this briefing does not cover

This briefing addresses secure tenancies only. It does not cover introductory tenancies
under section 124 of the Housing Act 1996, which follow a separate mandatory regime with a
statutory review; assured tenancies granted by housing associations, where the Housing Act
1988 grounds — including the mandatory Ground 8 — apply; nor possession of temporary or
homelessness accommodation, which turns on the basis of occupation rather than on Schedule
2.

## Currency and pending reform

The position stated here is settled law. The Renters' Rights Act 2025 principally reforms
assured tenancies in the private and housing-association sectors and does not displace the
secure-tenancy regime under the Housing Act 1985; its interaction with social-landlord
practice is under review. This briefing is next due for review on 1 October 2026, or
sooner if commencement regulations are made before that date.
